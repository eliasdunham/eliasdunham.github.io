MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · Mar–Jun 2026

High-pressure thermal characterization apparatus

Designed and built a pressure-vessel test rig from scratch to compare insulation materials under simulated depth. Integrated a PID-controlled heating element, digital multimeter logging, and electrical feedthroughs that hold seal under pressurization, then established the standardized protocol for pressurization, thermal conditioning, and cool-down measurement. Used the rig to benchmark experimental insulation materials against the current industry standard, and trained other lab members to operate the apparatus independently.

MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · 2026

In-situ mixing syringe

Dual-barrel syringe that keeps two formulation components isolated until they combine at the tip. Modeled the barrel, plunger, and gaskets in Fusion 360 and configured the tip geometry to confine mixing to the outlet. Ran a stress concentration analysis on the sealing interface to identify where the gaskets needed additional support, then revised the geometry to redistribute the sealing load and eliminate a persistent leak.

  • 11 design iterations
  • 23G needle validated
  • SLA printed

Northeastern University · Jan–Mar 2025

Biomimetic fan blade

Fan blade geometry derived from the mechanics of owl wings, aimed at reducing turbulence and noise for drone and HVAC applications. Incorporated ridges across the blade surface, mirroring the serrated feather structures owls rely on for silent flight. A smooth blade sheds large, spanwise-correlated vortices at the trailing edge, which is the dominant source of broadband noise; the ridges break those structures into smaller, less coherent vortices, shifting the acoustic energy upward in frequency and cutting how much of it radiates. Optimized the profile in SolidWorks using flow simulation, reaching a 30% noise reduction against a standard baseline design.

  • 30% noise reduction
  • Surface ridges
  • SolidWorks flow sim

MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · 2026

Electro-thermal actuation study

Characterization campaign across heater materials, power sources, and shape-memory alloy components to define the feasible power envelope for a millimeter-scale actuator. Built a controlled thermal-bath setup to measure alloy activation temperatures, then diagnosed and corrected a fixture-induced friction artifact that was corrupting the transition data. Mapped nichrome resistance against power across lengths and voltages and validated the results against Joule-heating theory.

  • Nitinol SMA
  • Silver-oxide cells
  • Joule-heating validated

Cell delivery catheter

Refined and precision-manufactured catheter components for intravenous delivery. Redesigned the central channel to eliminate a fluidic bottleneck and confirmed improved flow. Then produced the complete device sets the team ran experiments on, using a 3D printer and a rotary axis on a laser cutter to hit the required tolerances.

  • 40 devices
  • 320 parts
  • 8 parts per assembly

MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · Mar–Jun 2026

High-pressure thermal characterization apparatus

Designed and built a pressure-vessel test rig from scratch to compare insulation materials under simulated depth. Integrated a PID-controlled heating element, digital multimeter logging, and electrical feedthroughs that hold seal under pressurization, then established the standardized protocol for pressurization, thermal conditioning, and cool-down measurement. Benchmarked a phase-change material against standard neoprene. The rig is now run by other lab members I trained.

  • 45 psi max
  • 104 fsw equivalent
  • 3 trials per material

MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · 2026

In-situ mixing syringe

Dual-barrel syringe that keeps two formulation components isolated until they combine at the tip. Modeled the barrel, plunger, and gaskets in Fusion 360 and configured the tip geometry to confine mixing to the outlet. Traced a persistent leak to stress concentration at the corners of a half-circle gasket rather than treating it as an unavoidable defect, then revised the geometry to redistribute the sealing load.

  • 11 design iterations
  • 23G needle validated
  • SLA printed

MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · 2026

Electro-thermal actuation study

Characterization campaign across heater materials, power sources, and shape-memory alloy components to define the feasible power envelope for a millimeter-scale actuator. Built a controlled thermal-bath setup to measure alloy activation temperatures, then diagnosed and corrected a fixture-induced friction artifact that was corrupting the transition data. Mapped nichrome resistance against power across lengths and voltages and validated the results against Joule-heating theory.

  • Nitinol SMA
  • Silver-oxide cells
  • Joule-heating validated

MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · 2026

Cell delivery catheter

Redesigned and precision-manufactured catheter components for intravenous delivery. Found a taper in the central channel creating a fluidic bottleneck, resolved it in a single redesign to restore consistent channel diameter, and confirmed improved flow. Then produced the complete device sets the team ran experiments on, using a 3D printer and a rotary axis on a laser cutter to hit the required tolerances.

  • 40 devices
  • 320 parts
  • 8 parts per assembly

MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · Feb–Apr 2026

Wearable stimulator housing

Independently designed and fabricated a clear-resin enclosure for an active stimulator carried on a live animal during in-vivo trials. Contoured the geometry to the animal's back for wearability and dialed in tight tolerances around the wire ports and a snap-fit lid to resolve fluid ingress. Specified clear resin so the board stayed visible for inspection. The housing stayed sealed and functional throughout trials.

  • ~15 iterations
  • Snap-fit seal
  • Clear SLA resin

MIT Lab for Translational Engineering · 2026

Polymer composite characterization

Calculated filler-to-polymer ratios and fabricated a controlled sample set spanning seven formulations and three particle sizes. Optimized the process to produce consistent, defect-free parts by comparing material extrusion against injection molding and tuning pre-heat temperature, dwell time, and mold-release strategy. Designed and printed temperature-resistant molds across five revisions, then hardness-tested every formulation.

  • 7 ratios
  • 3 particle sizes
  • ~30 samples

Northeastern University · Jan–Mar 2025

Biomimetic fan blade

Fan blade geometry derived from the mechanics of owl wings, aimed at reducing turbulence and noise for drone and HVAC applications. Optimized the profile in SolidWorks using flow simulation, reaching a 30% noise reduction against a standard baseline design.

  • 30% noise reduction
  • SolidWorks flow sim

Northeastern University · Nov–Dec 2024

Motion tracker

Wearable device measuring acceleration and velocity in horse racing. Designed and fabricated the housing in SolidWorks and FDM print, integrated the sensors with Arduino, and wrote the MATLAB validation scripts that checked output against commercial units.

  • Under 5% deviation
  • Arduino
  • FDM printed
